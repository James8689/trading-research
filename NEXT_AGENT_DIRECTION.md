# Direction for the next trading-strategy research pass

James wants the next pass to be creatively original rather than a repetition of published internet strategies. The completed tests are a baseline and a set of eliminated ideas, not a ceiling. The working theory is that widely published rules are competed away or too well known to retain much edge by themselves.

Use first-principles reasoning and broad pretrained financial knowledge to generate genuinely new hypotheses. Start by asking why a trade might pay: who is forced to buy or sell, what information arrives with delay, what constraint prevents larger participants from harvesting the opportunity, and why the effect could survive realistic execution costs. Explore unusual combinations of market structure, accounting fundamentals, institutional flows, corporate actions, tax or settlement constraints, cross-asset transmission, and operational behavior. AI should be used for hypothesis generation, feature construction, and falsification only when it adds measurable alpha. The eventual live rule should be deterministic and inexpensive unless testing proves an always-on AI decision loop adds alpha after its cost and failure risk.

Do not merely add thresholds to moving averages, RSI, breakouts, volume or momentum. Do not select the best historical variant and call it validated. Each idea needs a written mechanism before data inspection, a data-availability audit, point-in-time inputs, no-lookahead code, realistic spread/slippage/latency assumptions, development and untouched validation periods, concentration analysis, and a small live quote or shadow-order test before any build or trade. Preserve failed ideas and checkpoint progress so another agent can resume.

Keep the current constraints unless James changes them: approximately $2,500 starting capital, $1,000–$5,000 range, about 25% drawdown ceiling, low-cost execution, and no discretionary management. Alpaca is the preferred API-native execution path; Robinhood's current official Agentic/MCP product is documented but standalone deterministic execution remains untested. No strategy in the current archive is approved for live money. The earnings-surprise idea in the handoff is only one possible next hypothesis and may be rejected if a stronger mechanism is found.
